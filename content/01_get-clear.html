// tool_02 · beginner_roadmap

The 5 non-negotiables before your first pitch.

Not optional. If even one of these is missing, the pitch will fail — not because the pitch is bad, but because the foundation isn't there yet.

01
A portfolio with 3+ pieces in one niche.

Not variety. Depth. Three pieces of skincare content is worth more than one skincare, one food, one tech. Brands book specialists. If you don't have 3, create spec work for a brand you love before you pitch anyone.

02
A rate you'll actually hold.

Run the Rate Calculator. Write your floor — the lowest you'll ever accept. Then add 25% for your opening quote. You need both numbers memorized before you talk to a brand.

03
One clear way to contact you.

A direct email. Not a form, not a DM-only account, not a booking link buried three clicks deep. One link that takes them directly to your inbox. Paste this block at the top of your portfolio and in your pitch signature:

Direct email: hello@[yourname].com  ·  Reply within 24 hrs  ·  Currently booking [Month] projects.
04
A proposal template on deck.

Before you pitch, have the Proposal Template filled in with your name and standard rates. When a brand says yes, you send paperwork inside 2 hours — not 2 days. Momentum dies in the gap.

05
A Client CRM set up and empty.

Get your Client CRM running — pick Brkaway (recommended), Notion, or Airtable. Full setup in 05_get-consistent. The moment you pitch someone, they go in the CRM — stage: pitched. This is how you run a real business, not a hobby.

// tool_03 · portfolio_checklist

The Portfolio Checklist.

What your portfolio actually needs to book higher-ticket deals. Score yours out of 12. If you're under 8, that's the first thing to fix.

portfolio.audit() score: 0 / 12

// scoring

  • 10–12: Book with confidence. Your floor should go up.
  • 7–9: Solid. Fix the gaps and your close rate jumps.
  • 4–6: You're leaving money on the table. Prioritize the unchecked items this month.
  • 0–3: Don't pitch yet. Pick the 3 highest-leverage gaps and spend the next 7 days closing them. You'll feel the difference inside one week — and your reply rate will be 3x what it would have been.

// tool_04 · offer_positioning

How to position your offer.

The difference between "I make videos" and "I make performance UGC for DTC beauty brands that drives paid results." One of those gets you $200 deals. The other gets you $2,000 ones.

// the_wrong_way

"Hi, I'm a content creator who does videos for brands."

"I do UGC, photography, social media management, and influencer work."

"My rates are flexible depending on what you need."

// vague + broad = low rates + bad clients

// the_right_way

"I make performance UGC for wellness brands — talking-head and testimonial style, built for paid social."

"My deliverable is simple: 3 × 30-second verticals, 2 concepts each, with 30 days usage rights for paid social."

"I turn around first drafts in 5 business days, I'm easy to give feedback to, and I don't disappear after you pay."

// specific + clear process = brands that trust you before they've even worked with you

// the_formula

Your offer sentence has 4 parts. This goes in your bio, your media kit, and your email signature — not your first pitch. Rates come in message two. See → pitch_style for how that conversation goes.

01 · what_you_make

Specific deliverable format (not "content" — "30-second vertical videos")

02 · who_you_make_it_for

Your niche + the type of brand (DTC, indie, enterprise, etc.)

03 · what_outcome_it_drives

Paid social results, organic reach, conversion, launch performance — one clear output

04 · what_makes_working_with_you_easy

Turnaround time, feedback process, communication style. Brands pay more for certainty — give them it.

// tool_05 · how_to_pitch

How to actually pitch a brand.

The goal of your first message is not to close a deal — it's to get a reply. Keep it short, keep it warm, and don't mention rates. That comes in message two, after they express interest.

// message_01 · the opener · no rates

Hey [Name]!

I've been following [Brand] for a while and I genuinely love what you've been putting out — especially [specific product / recent campaign / post you noticed]. Your content feels really different from what most brands in this space are doing.

I'm a UGC creator in the [niche] space and I think my style would be a great fit for you. Would love to connect if you're open to it!

[Your name]

✓ short — 4 sentences max
✓ specific — reference one real thing about them
✓ warm — sounds like a human, not a template
✗ no rates
✗ no portfolio link yet
✗ no deliverables list

// message_02 · when they reply · now rates

So glad you're interested!

I mainly do [style] UGC — here's my portfolio: [link]. My standard package is [X videos] for $[rate], which includes [usage rights]. Totally happy to adjust based on what you have in mind.

Want me to put together a quick proposal?

[Your name]

✓ portfolio link — now it lands with context
✓ specific rate — one number, not a range
✓ usage rights mentioned upfront
✓ proposal offer — moves to next step fast

// why_this_works

Most creators lead with rates because they're scared of "wasting time." But a cold email with a price list attached gets deleted immediately — the brand has no reason to care about your number before they care about you. Warm them up first. Get the reply. Then go to business.

One exception: if a brand reaches out to you first, you can mention rates in reply #1. They opted in. The dynamic is already different.
