    // tool_02 · negotiation_playbook

    What do I actually say?

    Pick the situation you're in. You'll get the exact script to send, the reasoning behind it, and the next move to make.

    est. 1 min
    negotiation_playbook.select() 6_scenarios
    01 "They want usage rights but won't raise the budget."
    +

    // script · copy this

    Happy to expand usage on this one. A couple of options depending on how you want to run it:

    → Organic only is $[BASE].
    → Adding 30 days of paid social is $[BASE × 1.4].
    → Full 90-day paid window is $[BASE × 1.7].

    Usage is the biggest lever on my side because it multiplies where this content lives. Which window works best for your launch timing?

    // why_this_works

    You're not saying no — you're reframing the conversation around scope. Giving them three tiers makes the "organic only" option feel restrictive and the middle option feel reasonable. They'll almost always pick paid-30.

    // next_move

    If they still push back, offer a 10% discount on paid-30 in exchange for an extended relationship (2 more deliverables this quarter). Never cut the base rate to unlock usage — unbundle it instead.

    02 "They lowballed my quote."
    +

    // script · copy this

    Thanks for the transparency on the budget. My rate for this scope is $[YOUR RATE] based on [1–2 line reason: usage, turnaround, concepts]. I can't go lower on the base — but I can adjust what's inside the package to fit the $[THEIR BUDGET] number.

    At that budget, I'd recommend dropping to [smaller scope] which still gives you [core outcome they wanted]. Want me to send that version?

    // why_this_works

    Protecting your rate protects every future rate you ever quote. Adjusting scope instead of discounting teaches the brand that your number is a number — not a starting point. You keep your floor intact and they still feel heard.

    // next_move

    If they accept the smaller scope, upsell on turnaround or revisions before you close. If they decline everything, walk. A brand that can't afford your floor won't refer you to one that can.

    03 "Scope is creeping mid-project."
    +

    // script · copy this

    Totally doable! Quick heads up — [the new ask] is outside the original scope we agreed to in the SOW. Happy to add it on as an extra at $[ADD-ON RATE], or we can swap it in for [one of the existing deliverables] at no extra cost. Which way would you prefer?

    // why_this_works

    Neutral, zero-blame language. "Happy to do it" keeps the relationship warm. The swap option makes it feel collaborative — but either answer keeps your rate protected. Most brands pick the add-on because they've already scoped the launch.

    // next_move

    Whatever they pick, confirm it in writing in the same thread before you do the work. Reference the original SOW line explicitly. Your proposal template in section_02 has a scope-change clause you can paste.

    04 "Payment is late."
    +

    // script · day_3_past_due

    Hey [name], just a quick check-in — invoice #[NUMBER] for $[AMOUNT] was due [DATE]. Can you confirm it's in the queue? Happy to resend if it got lost.

    // script · day_10_past_due

    Following up on invoice #[NUMBER] — it's now 10 days past due. Per our SOW, a 5% late fee has been added and is reflected in the attached updated invoice. Please confirm expected payment date.

    // why_this_works

    Two-step escalation. Day 3 is warm and reasonable — assumes the best. Day 10 is firm, references the contract, and activates the fee. Never threaten without the clause in your SOW. Every proposal you send from here on should include a late-fee clause. The template in section_02 already has one.

    // next_move

    After day 14, pause any ongoing deliverables and CC the brand's finance contact. Don't deliver more work until the invoice clears.

    05 "They're asking for unlimited revisions."
    +

    // script · copy this

    I include 2 rounds of revisions in my standard package because that's what it takes to get to a strong final cut without diluting it. Additional revisions after round 2 are billed at $50/round. If you're expecting more than 2 because of internal review cycles, totally fine — I'd just adjust the quote upfront so we're aligned from day one.

    // why_this_works

    Unlimited revisions is the single fastest way to turn a profitable deal into an hourly-wage loss. Capping at 2 and charging per-round after is fair — the brand either respects the cap or signals upfront that they have a messy review process (which you should price in).

    // next_move

    Put this directly in your proposal template's "what's included" block. Don't negotiate revisions on a per-deal basis — set the rule once, apply it every time.

    06 "They want exclusivity in the category."
    +

    // script · copy this

    Category exclusivity is something I price separately because it limits who I can work with for the duration. For [CATEGORY], a 90-day exclusive adds 40% to the base, 6 months adds 75%, and 12 months is a 2x multiplier. It also comes with a signed non-compete clause. Which window works for your campaign?

    // why_this_works

    Exclusivity is real money on the line — every competitor deal you turn down during that window is income you're losing. Charging for it protects your pipeline and signals that you're thinking about this like a business. Brands that can't pay for exclusivity rarely enforce it anyway.

    // next_move

    If they balk, offer a "no direct competitor" clause instead (only blocks 3 named brands) for a smaller premium. It's a middle ground that keeps them happy and your pipeline free.
