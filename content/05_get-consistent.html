The Proposal Template.

The exact structure I send after a pitch lands. Short, scannable, and scoped so nothing creeps later.

expand proposal.md +

1. Project Summary

[YOUR NAME] × [BRAND NAME] — UGC content for [PRODUCT / LAUNCH]. [1-line on the objective: drive paid performance, seed a launch, refresh organic, etc.]

2. Deliverables

  • Format: [e.g. 3 × 30-second vertical videos, 2 concepts each]
  • Concepts: 2 unique hooks per video. A-roll + B-roll, edited and captioned.
  • Revisions: 2 rounds of revisions included per deliverable. Additional rounds billed at $50/round.
  • Raw footage: Delivered alongside final cuts via Google Drive or Dropbox.

3. Usage Rights

  • Organic: Unlimited organic use on [brand's owned channels].
  • Paid social: 30 days across Meta + TikTok, from delivery date.
  • Exclusions: Out-of-home, TV, print, paid YouTube, and whitelisting are not included in this scope and are quoted separately.

4. Timeline

  • Day 0: Contract signed, 50% deposit paid.
  • Day 3: Brief + product received. Concepts locked.
  • Day 10: First drafts delivered.
  • Day 14: Final cuts delivered after revisions.

5. Investment

  • Total: $[TOTAL RATE] USD.
  • Structure: 50% deposit to begin, 50% on final delivery.
  • Late fee: Invoices more than 10 days past due accrue a 5% late fee per the terms of this SOW.
  • Kill fee: If the project is cancelled after concepts are approved, 50% of the project fee is retained.

6. Scope Changes

Any requests outside the scope above will be quoted as an add-on before work begins. I'll always confirm scope changes in writing before invoicing for them.

7. What I'll Need From You

  • Product(s) shipped by [DATE]
  • Brand brief with any must-hit talking points and any "no-go" language
  • A single point of contact for feedback and approvals

8. Next Step

If this works, reply with a "yes" and I'll send the contract and invoice for the 50% deposit. Excited to build this with you.

[YOUR NAME]
[YOUR EMAIL]

// how_to_use

  1. Set it up once in Notion / Airtable / Sheets using the column names above.
  2. Every time you send an outbound pitch, add a row. Stage: pitched. Pitched_date: today.
  3. Review the CRM every Monday for 15 minutes. Update next_action on every active row.
  4. Filter on stage = invoiced every Friday to check for late payments.
  5. Filter on usage_expires within 30 days every month — those are warm re-engagement opportunities, not cold outreach.
  6. Filter on niche when planning your next outbound batch — go deep on one category at a time.

// deal_goes_wrong · decision_tree

When something stalls, use the stage in your CRM to know exactly what to do next:

pitched
No reply in 5 days → send follow_up_01. No reply in 10 days → send follow_up_02. No reply in 14 days → archive and re-pitch in 60 days. Scripts in 04.
proposal_sent
No reply in 3 days → one short check-in: "Wanted to make sure this landed — any questions on the scope?" No reply in 7 days → politely withdraw and re-offer 30 days out.
signed
Deposit not received in 5 business days → follow up once. Not received in 10 → send a single message: "I have you on my schedule for [week]. I'll hold your spot until [DATE] — just need the deposit to confirm." Release the date if nothing arrives.
invoiced
Past due 5 days → friendly reminder. Past due 10 days → formal notice with late fee language from your contract. Past due 30 days → final demand. After that, collections or small claims. Keep every email.
in_production
Brand goes silent on feedback → set a hard deadline: "I need your notes by [DATE] to hit the delivery date. If I don't hear back I'll proceed with my best judgment." Then deliver on the original date regardless.

// template_03 · onboarding_workflow

The Onboarding Workflow.

A 7-step checklist for the first 72 hours of every signed deal. Run it every time.

onboarding.run() 7_steps · 72_hrs
  1. 01
    Send contract + invoice within 2 hours of verbal yes.

    Momentum dies in the gap between "yes" and paperwork. Use a pre-built contract (HelloSign, PandaDoc) and a pre-built invoice. Both should be warm-ready before the call.

  2. 02
    Confirm deposit before any work begins.

    No deposit, no concepts, no shoot date. This is not negotiable. If the brand balks, you've caught a red flag early and saved yourself a problem.

  3. 03
    Send the creative brief form.

    One form (Google Form or Typeform) that asks: target audience, top 3 messages, brand voice do's and don'ts, references they like, references they hate, any compliance/legal constraints.

  4. 04
    Confirm product shipping details in writing.

    Address, ETA, tracking. Add the ETA to your calendar as a hard milestone. No product, no concepts — you'll push delivery accordingly.

  5. 05
    Set up a shared delivery folder.

    Google Drive or Dropbox. Name it [BRAND] × [YOUR NAME] — [MONTH YEAR]. Pre-create: 01_brief, 02_concepts, 03_drafts, 04_final, 05_raw.

  6. 06
    Identify your creative POC — and your AP/finance contact separately.

    One person on the brand side owns feedback and approvals. If you're getting conflicting notes from three stakeholders, pause and politely ask who the final decision-maker is.

    // ap_contact_script

    "Quick admin question — who should I send the invoice to when it's time? Just want to make sure the right person has it from the start." Send this within 24 hours of the signed contract. Don't wait until delivery. Chasing payment through the wrong person adds 2-3 weeks to your collections timeline.

  7. 07
    Block the shoot + delivery dates on your calendar today.

    Before you take another inbound inquiry. Overbooking is the fastest way to miss a deadline and burn a relationship.

// faq · bookkeeping + legal

Should I form an LLC before I start charging? +
Short answer: not immediately. Operate as a sole proprietor under your own name until you're consistently clearing ~$30K/year from UGC work. At that point, an LLC (with an S-corp election in the US) starts saving you meaningful tax dollars. Before that, the paperwork and state fees aren't worth it. Disclaimer: I'm not an accountant — this is just what I did. Talk to one before you file anything.
How do I handle taxes as a creator? +
Set aside 25–30% of every deposit into a separate high-yield savings account labeled "taxes" and don't touch it. File quarterly estimated taxes if you're in the US and earning more than $1K/quarter from UGC. Hire an accountant who has at least 3 other creator clients — it's worth the $400–800/year.

// 5_rules_of_redlining

01

Your redlines are not negotiating — they're correcting. Standard creator protections already exist. You're asking for what's normal. Don't apologize for it.

02

Send redlines fast. A contract sitting in your inbox unsigned is a deal that can dissolve for nothing. Get your notes back within 24 hours of receiving it.

03

Prioritize high severity first. If they reject a high-severity redline, that conversation has to happen before anything else moves forward. You need to make a call.

04

Don't explain your redlines — state them. "Here's the standard language for this clause." Not "I was wondering if maybe we could..." You know what you're asking for.

05

If they refuse all redlines on a high-severity clause, that's data. Walk, or sign with your eyes open — not both. You chose.
