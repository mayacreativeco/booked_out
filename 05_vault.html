// vault_03 · swipe_file

Swipe file: pitches that closed.

Real outbound emails I've sent that closed deals between $1,800 and $6,500. Names redacted. Everything else is verbatim.

swipe_01 · closed_$2400
The one-line hook opener.
+
Subject: quick idea for your [launch] campaign

Hi [Name],

Saw your [launch] go live on Instagram last week — the positioning around [specific thing] is the strongest I've seen in the [niche] category this quarter.

Quick idea: a 3-piece GRWM package, each with a different "first 30 seconds" hook, aimed at your top-of-funnel TikTok audience. I can have a concept deck to you inside 48 hours if you're interested.

Starting at $1,800 for the package with 30 days paid usage.

— [Your name]

// why_it_worked

Specific compliment anchored to something recent. Clear deliverable. Real number. Concept deck as the CTA (low-pressure for them, high-value for me). Closed in 3 emails over 8 days.

swipe_02 · closed_$4100
The results-led pitch.
+
Subject: a creator who hit 3.2 ROAS for [comparable brand]

Hey [Name],

My UGC work for [redacted wellness brand] hit a 3.2x ROAS at an $8 CPA running on Meta + TikTok through Q1. I think the same framework would land for [their brand], specifically for [a product line].

Quick Loom here — it's 2 minutes and walks through the 3 concepts I'd build: [Loom link]

If it's interesting, my starting package is $3,900 for 3 × 30-second verticals with 30 days paid usage. Happy to scope up or down.

— [Your name]

// why_it_worked

Lead with the number. Subject line is doing all the work — they had to click. Loom link made the follow-up asynchronous. They upgraded to 4 videos on the reply, which pushed it from $3,900 to $4,100.

The referral-adjacent pitch.
+
Subject: [Mutual] mentioned you're thinking about creator content

Hi [Name],

[Mutual's first name] at [previous brand] mentioned you're actively spinning up creator content for Q2. She hired me for the [previous project] and said I should reach out directly.

I put together a quick brief based on what I saw in your last 3 launches — specifically, a 5-piece package (2 hero videos, 2 photo sets, 1 testimonial) built for paid social. Happy to walk you through it on a 15-min call this week.

Warm intro beats cold outbound 9 times out of 10. The brief was pre-built, so I was pitching a specific thing, not asking "are you interested in creator content". Referral gave me permission to quote higher. Closed on the first reply.

// vault_04 · operator_faq

Operator FAQ.

The weird questions I get asked most — the ones that don't fit anywhere else but matter when you're running the business.

Should I form an LLC before I start charging? +
Short answer: not immediately. Operate as a sole proprietor under your own name until you're consistently clearing ~$30K/year from UGC work. At that point, an LLC (with an S-corp election in the US) starts saving you meaningful tax dollars. Before that, the paperwork and state fees aren't worth it. Disclaimer: I'm not an accountant — this is just what I did. Talk to one before you file anything.
Do I need a contract for every deal? +
Yes. Every single one. Even the $500 ones. The proposal template in section_02 can double as a lightweight SOW when signed. Use a tool like HelloSign or PandaDoc to make it one-click on their side.
How do I handle taxes as a creator? +
Set aside 25-30% of every deposit into a separate high-yield savings account labeled "taxes" and don't touch it. File quarterly estimated taxes if you're in the US and earning more than $1K/quarter from UGC. Hire an accountant who has at least 3 other creator clients — it's worth the $400-800/year.
What if a brand wants me to use their product in a way I don't believe in? +
Walk. Every single time. The content won't perform (because your delivery will be off), the brand will blame you, and your audience will sniff it out. Your portfolio is worth more than any one deal. The recovery scripts include a polite exit note you can send.
How do I handle a brand that keeps scope-creeping after signing? +
Use the scope-creep script in the Negotiation Playbook (scenario 03). Frame every new request as an add-on or a swap — never as "free extra work". One warning then the add-on invoice goes out regardless. Brands respect clear boundaries.
When should I raise my rates? +
When more than 70% of your pitches are converting, it's time to raise. High conversion = you're under-priced. Raise the floor by 20-25% and watch what happens. You'll lose the cheapest deals and keep the ones that actually move the business. Re-run the rate calculator under "established" to see your new numbers.
